Shared parser: month first for day-first tenants

The shared parser treats dates as MM/DD while the affected tenants write DD/MM. App and email reports, and booking-v2 form reports, belong here. For example, R-41200 reports a 7 April reminder sent for 4 July. Use an explicit unambiguous input contract and tenant-aware parsing; test ambiguous dates, invalid month/day values, and app/email/form paths before marking fixed.

Booking-v3: wrong date hint

Booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY even for a day-first tenant, while the form reads dates in that tenant’s order. People following the hint get a different booking date. This affects form reports from alder.physio, copper.studio, and pale.harbour, not every report from those tenants. R-41230 and R-41241 illustrate this path. Make the hint agree with the tenant’s actual input format and validate with an end-to-end submission test. Keep this ticket separate from the parser.

What not to infer

Browser locale and intake tags are guesses, not tenant configuration. Subject text is not a cause key. The five reports listed in desk-notes.json describe a one-day move, but the supplied tenant-based logic files them under parser; the cause table footnotes them there. Do not count them twice or invent a confirmed timezone bug. Preserve their IDs for follow-up on that symptom.

The desk kept working

The received and merged series measure different events. A batch of merges is not a burst of new failures. Reports continued to arrive after the initial merge batch through the export cutoff: “known” did not stop customer impact. The later engineering update closes the incident with different outcomes for each cause. For future reports, re-run the grouping for new exports, update each ticket’s unique report count, and route by cause instead of folding them into the biggest ticket. A new failure after closure needs fresh investigation, not an assumption that both causes received a code fix.
